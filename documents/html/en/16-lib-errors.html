16. Error Handling

Every call returns one of three outcomes, and every error says whether the car may have acted and whether trying again could help. The library already retries what is safe to retry, so most apps only need to branch on the outcome.

Three outcomes

OutcomeMeaningWhat your app does
SuccessThe car confirmedUse the value
UncertainA command: the car may have done it, but no confirmation arrived. A query: no data was confirmed, and nothing was done (getState results)A command: do not send it again, read the state instead (vehicleStatus(), or getState() for infotainment state). A query: keep the last value and ask again later
FailureIt did not happenLook at error

In Swift, switch onEnum(of: result) covers .success, .uncertain and .failure; see 12. Getting Started. Cancelling your own coroutine or Task is not turned into an outcome; the cancellation propagates as usual.

Error types

Every VehicleError carries a message (English), mayHaveSucceeded and temporary. shouldRetry() is true when the error is temporary and the car cannot have acted. The library already retries those, so you do not need a retry loop. One exception: KeyStoreError.Locked is temporary, but the library never retries it, because only the user can unlock the phone; call again after the unlock.

A code in that snapshot is now reported with its named error type instead of an Unknown… type with a number. Whether the library retries does not change. getState results

A query changes nothing on the car. So Uncertain on a read means no data was confirmed, not that a command may have run. mayHaveSucceeded is still true, because reads and commands share the error types. There is nothing to check afterwards: keep the last value and ask again later. Asking again is safe, but not in a tight loop; the library sets no interval, so it is your app's choice.

ResultMeaningWhat your app does
Success(data)The car answeredCheck every field you use for null. data can be empty, and a success is not proof of a new measurement: compare timestamp with the previous reply (14, A8.3)
Uncertain(ProtocolFault(RESPONSE_MTU_EXCEEDED))The car received the request but the reply was too large. The car is reachableKeep the last value with its age; see the next section
Uncertain(Timeout(afterSend = true))Nothing came back in time: infotainment is asleep, the reply was lost, or the link dropped while waitingRead vehicleStatus(). ASLEEP: your app may wake the car (14, A8.2). Otherwise keep the last value with its age. Check connection too
Uncertain(BadResponse(detail))A reply arrived but could not be readKeep the last value. Report detail if it repeats
Failure(Timeout(afterSend = false))Time ran out before sending or while waiting to retry, for example when the car keeps answering busy while it wakesAsk again later, with a longer timeout right after a wake
Failure(NoSession)No infotainment session (nothing was sent), or the car did not accept the saved onestartSession(setOf(VehicleDomain.INFOTAINMENT)), then ask again once
Failure(InfotainmentRejected(reason))The car refused. This wins over any data in the replyShow a generic message. reason is the car's own text; do not log it
Failure(NotConnected), TransportErrorThe link is gone or the write failedCall disconnect(), then connect again (below)

Any other error means what the error type table above says. Your interface can tell the two main Uncertain cases apart by error type: a size fault means the car answered, a timeout means nothing came back. Neither is a reason to clear the session.

Response size limit on getState

The car sends no data when its reply goes over a size limit. The largest reply seen on real cars is about 453 bytes; this is an observed value, not a specification. getState() returns Uncertain(ProtocolFault(RESPONSE_MTU_EXCEEDED)), and the app cannot make the reply smaller.

It is most likely on Drive. A long destination name makes it more frequent, with reports of failures lasting tens of seconds, and of one Model S/X build (2026.27.6.15, South Korea) failing almost always even with no route.

The library does not resend the request. It does not clear the session, reconnect or wake the car because of this fault; session information that came with the reply is still applied as usual. The fault does not mean the car is gone or asleep. Keep the last value with its age, show it as unavailable when it is too old, and do not retry in a tight loop. Live speed is not guaranteed, and how this behaves per firmware is not confirmed on a real car (#118).

Real-car evidence (Likely, not from our own getState): in one session replies to another Bluetooth client reached our link (14, B3.5), and 132 of its 1690 infotainment frames carried MESSAGEFAULT_ERROR_RESPONSE_MTU_EXCEEDED. They were 106-byte frames with no payload and the fault in plaintext signedMessageStatus. All 132 fell within about 11 minutes of one connection, in runs of one or two, and most runs were followed at once by a session_info reply; the next connection had none. The largest frame in that session was 453 bytes (350 bytes of ciphertext). What the client asked for is unknown: its requests are not visible and the payloads are encrypted.

Reply authenticity

Do not base safety decisions on getState

Like the Go original, the library accepts a plaintext reply, because firmware before 2024.38 does not encrypt responses (vehicle-command/pkg/protocol/protocol.md:751). A plaintext reply carries no authentication tag, so a party on the BLE link could forge read data. The same goes for vehicleStatus() and vehicle.events, which are unauthenticated plaintext by design (14, A1, B1). Command replies are not exempt: the plaintext path has no domain check, so a forged plaintext VCSEC reply can make a command such as lock() return Success even if the car never ran it. Planned, not implemented yet (ADR-0015): authenticated commands will need a verified encrypted reply to succeed, and firmware before 2024.38 will no longer be supported.

Broadcast events

vehicle.events has no error values and never throws. It ends normally when the link drops or disconnect() is called (14. Reading Vehicle State, B2); why the link dropped is in connection.

Not an error: no VehicleError, and the flow keeps runningKeep the collector light and hand slow work to another coroutine. The car resends its status, but that can take 60 s while awake with a person present and was not measured otherwise (14, B3.1). To get the state sooner, read vehicleStatus()

Queries while another Bluetooth client is active

Status: Likely, from real-car logs of one car (14, B3.5). It was not reproduced in a controlled scenario, and the mechanism is unknown.

While another Bluetooth client was using the car, its infotainment replies reached our link at about one frame per second and queries could get no reply at all:

SessionOther client's infotainment frameslistKeys()vehicleStatus(), keySummary()
With that traffic, 53 polls16906 Success, 47 Uncertain(Timeout(afterSend = true))106 of 106 Success
Without it, 37 polls037 of 37 Success74 of 74 Success
  • This is the ordinary lost-reply result: the request was sent and nothing came back. No late reply arrived after the timeout either, so a longer timeout is not expected to help.
  • listKeys() is the most exposed because it sends requests one after another, the summary plus one per occupied slot (eight requests in about 0.8 s with seven keys). Requests sent under 0.55 s after the other client's latest frame were all answered, and 45 of the 47 lost ones were sent 0.55 to 0.85 s after it. Send times are inferred from the previous reply, because the log has no transmit lines.
  • vehicleStatus() and keySummary() are not immune: earlier recordings with such traffic have 4 Uncertain(Timeout) results on them. Their causes are not established; one came 1.6 s before a link loss.
  • The link stayed up: the next poll's vehicleStatus() succeeded every time.
  • No command was sent during this traffic, so whether a command stalls the same way is unknown.
